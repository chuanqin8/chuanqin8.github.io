About

Hi! I am Chuan Qin (秦川), a Ph.D. candidate at the School of Information Science and Engineering, Lanzhou University, advised by Prof. Long Jin.

My research sits at the intersection of deep learning theory and embodied intelligence. I develop optimization algorithms that improve the generalization and efficiency of neural networks, and I apply these theoretical insights to robot control and decision-making. I am particularly interested in bridging dynamical systems theory and modern deep learning — understanding how training dynamics, sharpness-aware minimization, and gradient flow influence the generalization of large models. I believe that a principled understanding of optimization landscapes is key to building robust, efficient, and generalizable AI systems. I was deeply involved in projects funded by CCF-Huawei, CCF-Baidu, and CAAI-Huawei MindSpore. My work has appeared in Neural Networks, IEEE Transactions on Consumer Electronics, and IEEE/CAA Journal of Automatica Sinica. I serve as a reviewer for IEEE/CAA Journal of Automatica Sinica (JAS).

Beyond theory, I also build systems. I independently developed a desktop tidy-up embodied AI system on a Franka arm — from a self-built teleoperation data-collection SDK, through VLA post-training (SFT with IWR on StarVLA / Qwen3-VL-4B), to real-time closed-loop deployment that generalizes across arbitrary object poses (see the demo below). I am familiar with Transformer/VLM/VLA architectures (StarVLA, ACT, Diffusion Policy, OpenVLA) and have hands-on experience with SFT and DeepSpeed distributed training.

In my spare time, I am a half-marathon runner. Personal best: 1:42:38 at the 2024 Lanzhou Marathon.

Featured Project

Desktop Object Tidy-Up: An End-to-End Embodied VLA System
StarVLA (Qwen3-VL-4B + flow-matching action head) × Franka arm — full pipeline built independently
Real-Robot Spatial Generalization
  • Task. Autonomous recognition, grasping, and tidy-up of tabletop objects (canonical task: “put the Rubik's cube into the box”), with the full pipeline from data collection and training to real-robot deployment built independently.
  • Model. A vision-language-action (VLA) model built on the StarVLA framework — Qwen3-VL-4B vision-language backbone with a layer-wise flow-matching action head, end-to-end from scene understanding to action generation.
  • Data collection. A self-developed teleoperation SDK (AI-assisted development) records multi-view RGB images, joint states, and end-effector poses in sync to build the demonstration dataset.
  • Training. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with IWR importance weighting over demonstrations to boost policy generalization in the low-data regime.
  • Deployment. Real-time closed-loop inference on a Franka arm — the policy completes the task under arbitrary cube/box placements, showing strong spatial generalization.
